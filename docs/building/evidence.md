# Initial scientific evidence

Updated and links checked: 2026-10-03. This is a starting bibliography, not a systematic review or proof that Noctua will improve learning.

Unless noted in an entry, the references below were checked against the linked paper's abstract, bibliographic record, or author-hosted opening pages. Full methods, statistical results, and subsequent literature have not yet been critically reviewed. Product implications are explicitly our inferences.

## How we will use evidence

For a consequential learning decision, record the specific claim, study population and task, comparison, outcome and delay, limitations, and proposed application. Prefer original studies and relevant research syntheses over popular explanations. Record whether we read the abstract, full paper, or supplementary material.

Our research scope includes neuroscience, cognitive psychology, and education. The initial references primarily address observable learning behavior. A neural mechanism alone does not establish that a particular app interaction is effective. Avoid universal prescriptions about difficulty, retention percentages, or learning styles without relevant evidence.

“Desirable difficulty” should refer to a condition that benefits later learning, even if practice feels harder or performance initially falls. Difficulty or effort alone does not establish that benefit. For Noctua, matching challenge to the learner and checking later learning are related but distinct design tasks. See [E5](#e5-learning-versus-performance).

## E1: Retrieval practice

Roediger, H. L., III, & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). *Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention.* Psychological Science, 17(3), 249–255. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x); [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/).

- **Evidence:** Two experiments used studied prose passages. Retrieval without feedback produced better delayed retention than repeated study; the immediate comparison favored repeated study.
- **Design inference:** Provide opportunities to retrieve before looking at an answer and revisit material after a delay.
- **Limit:** This comparison does not establish that flashcards alone create deep STEM understanding or transfer. It also does not specify an optimal tutoring or feedback policy.

## E2: Distributed practice

Cepeda, N. J., Pashler, H., Vul, E., Wixted, J. T., & Rohrer, D. (2006). *Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis.* Psychological Bulletin, 132(3), 354–380. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354); [author-hosted paper](https://laplab.ucsd.edu/articles/Cepeda_etal_2006.pdf).

- **Evidence:** A quantitative synthesis of verbal recall experiments found that spacing and the final retention interval jointly affect later retention.
- **Design inference:** Make revisiting material easy and consider the intended time horizon when scheduling practice.
- **Limit:** Evidence for spacing does not validate a particular scheduler, its parameters, or a universal interval sequence. Applying it to complex problem solving requires additional evidence.

## E3: Self-explanation

Chi, M. T. H., de Leeuw, N., Chiu, M.-H., & LaVancher, C. (1994). *Eliciting self-explanations improves understanding.* Cognitive Science, 18(3), 439–477. [DOI and abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15516709cog1803_3); [author-hosted paper](https://education.asu.edu/sites/g/files/litvpz656/files/lcl/chideleeuwchiulavancher_3.pdf).

- **Evidence:** In a small study of eighth-grade students reading about the circulatory system, prompting self-explanations was associated with greater learning gains than reading the text twice.
- **Design inference:** Ask the student to explain mechanisms and connections, and use their explanation as the basis for feedback and collaborative notes.
- **Limit:** The small sample and specific text constrain generalization. This does not prove that every explanation prompt is useful or that our note workflow will improve understanding.

## E4: Worked examples and prior knowledge

Sweller, J., & Cooper, G. A. (1985). *The use of worked examples as a substitute for problem solving in learning algebra.* Cognition and Instruction, 2(1), 59–89. [DOI and publisher abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s1532690xci0201_3).

- **Evidence:** Algebra experiments found benefits from studying worked examples for subsequent problems with the same structure, including reduced solution time and errors.
- **Design inference:** Consider sufficient instruction and examples when the learner lacks a usable starting model, followed by their own explanation and practice.
- **Limit:** The reported benefits were specific to structurally similar problems. This does not justify handing over the answer to the student's current task or guarantee broad transfer. It challenges treating unguided struggle as universally beneficial.

## E5: Learning versus performance

Soderstrom, N. C., & Bjork, R. A. (2015). *Learning versus performance: An integrative review.* Perspectives on Psychological Science, 10(2), 176–199. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615569000); [author-hosted paper](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/11/soderstorm_ra_learningvsperformance.pdf).

- **Evidence:** This review distinguishes performance during acquisition from learning assessed through later retention and transfer. The two can diverge.
- **Design inference:** Assess later independent performance and retain task context rather than treating in-session fluency or confidence as mastery.
- **Limit:** This is an integrative review, not a validated learner model or a formula for choosing difficulty. Noctua's proposed dimensions of understanding need their own assessment design.

## E6: Formative feedback

Shute, V. J. (2008). *Focus on formative feedback.* Review of Educational Research, 78(1), 153–189. [DOI and abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/0034654307313795).

- **Evidence:** This review examines feedback intended to improve learning, including response verification, explanations, hints, and worked examples, with attention to timing and learner characteristics.
- **Design inference:** Give specific feedback on the student's work and choose the form of help according to the task and learner, rather than defaulting to praise or an unexplained grade.
- **Limit:** It does not establish that immediate feedback is always best or that an LLM's assessment is accurate. Model feedback must be checked against reliable sources and task criteria.

## E7: AI assistance and independent learning

Bastani, H., Bastani, O., Sungu, A., Ge, H., Kabakcı, Ö., & Mariman, R. (2025). *Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics.* Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 122(26), e2422633122. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122); [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40560616/).

- **Evidence:** A field experiment compared a general GPT interface and a tutor designed to protect learning. Both improved assisted practice performance; the general interface harmed later unaided performance, while the tutor's safeguards largely mitigated that harm.
- **Design inference:** Evaluate what the learner can do after assistance is removed. Treat tutoring design and answer availability as material product choices.
- **Limit:** This is evidence from a particular high-school mathematics setting and tool design, not a conclusion about all AI tutors. It does not establish that Noctua's proposed policy will be effective.

## E8: Retrieval and conceptual learning

Karpicke, J. D., & Blunt, J. R. (2011). *Retrieval practice produces more learning than elaborative studying with concept mapping.* Science, 331(6018), 772–775. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1199327); [author-hosted paper](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2011/2011_Karpicke_Blunt_Science.pdf).

- **Read scope:** Abstract and selected main-text methods/results, particularly the comparison and one-week comprehension/inference test. Supplementary methods and full subsequent literature have not been reviewed.
- **Evidence:** Retrieval practice with science texts benefited later comprehension and inference in the studied comparisons.
- **Design inference:** Recall and conceptual work can overlap; classify observations by what they test rather than assigning every card exclusively to memory and every exercise exclusively to understanding.
- **Limit:** This does not validate Noctua's concept ranks/evidence model, every retrieval format, or a claim that concept mapping is always inferior.

## E9: Knowledge tracing

Corbett, A. T., & Anderson, J. R. (1994, publisher issue date). *Knowledge tracing: Modeling the acquisition of procedural knowledge.* User Modeling and User-Adapted Interaction, 4, 253–278. [Publisher abstract and DOI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01099821).

- **Evidence:** In an ACT programming tutor, the model tracked estimates for component rules using student performance and studied their relation to test performance.
- **Design inference:** Track assessable components and update estimates from relevant observations. Consider knowledge tracing as a later probabilistic baseline.
- **Limit:** A structured programming tutor differs substantially from an open STEM vault and AI-generated tasks. This does not establish a general understanding score or suitable parameters for Noctua. The publisher lists 1994; some references list 1995.

## E10: Availability and accessibility

Tulving, E., & Pearlstone, Z. (1966). *Availability versus accessibility of information in memory for words.* Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 5(4), 381–391. [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371%2866%2980048-8).

- **Evidence:** In categorized-word recall, category cues improved recall over the uncued condition. The authors distinguished availability in storage from accessibility at retrieval.
- **Design inference:** A failed retrieval observation need not mean knowledge is entirely absent. Inspect cues and subsequent reasoning before interpreting the learner's gap.
- **Limit:** This word-list experiment does not establish that someone who forgets a STEM formula understands the topic. The application to conceptual scoring is our inference, requiring direct task evidence.

## E11: Transfer across contexts

Barnett, S. M., & Ceci, S. J. (2002). *When and where do we apply what we learn? A taxonomy for far transfer.* Psychological Bulletin, 128(4), 612–637. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.128.4.612); [original abstract and bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12081085/).

- **Read scope:** The original abstract and bibliographic record, verified on 2026-10-03. The publisher full text was unavailable; the full review has not been read.
- **Evidence/context:** This review proposes multiple dimensions for describing transfer and classifying earlier studies. Its abstract reports substantial evidence under some conditions while important conditions remain untested; treating far transfer as one undifferentiated effect is misleading.
- **Design inference:** Specify what changed between learning and an assessment context. Two different task IDs or an AI `transfer` label cannot establish flexible use in very different settings.
- **Limit:** This is a framework/review, not validation of Noctua's five ranks, generated tasks, or candidate thresholds. The full classification and underlying studies need review before adopting detailed transfer criteria.

## How the first implementation uses this evidence

The experiment provides retrieve-before-reveal cards (E1), scheduled revisiting (E2), reasoning/explanation exercises (E3), explicit help history (E5/E7), and correctable feedback (E6). These are design inferences, not claims that the implementation reproduces the studies or improves learning. The five concept ranks, default FSRS parameters, and candidate thresholds are engineering/product choices without scientific validation. A prompt requesting a checked exercise does not establish its correctness.

## Research still needed

No full-paper review or additional educational-effectiveness claim is adopted. In particular, map navigation differs from reconstructing relationships, and E7's tutor used teacher-supplied problem information as well as answer-availability safeguards.

- Retrieval for conceptual understanding and transfer, beyond recall of prose or isolated facts.
- Adaptive support, fading of worked examples, productive failure, and the conditions under which difficulty helps or obstructs learning.
- Interleaving and learning to distinguish which STEM method to use.
- Assessment of reasoning, misconceptions, confidence calibration, and reliability of model grading.
- Student-generated notes, diagrams, mathematical representations, and multimedia learning.
- Cognitive neuroscience of learning and memory where it informs a concrete decision, with a clear link to behavioral outcomes.

For each area, first define the product question, then examine relevant syntheses and original studies. A bibliography should not become a list of impressive citations detached from actual behavior.
